## Procedure Development

A maintenance procedure should clearly define:

- Purpose
- Scope
- Applicable requirements
- Definitions
- Roles and responsibilities
- Required personnel
- Tools and equipment
- Materials
- Work sequence
- Inspection requirements
- Safety controls
- Quality requirements
- Documentation requirements
- Emergency response
- Acceptance criteria

The objective is to ensure that maintenance activities can be performed consistently, safely, and traceably.

## Preventive Maintenance Program

Preventive maintenance planning considers:

- Asset type
- Asset criticality
- Inspection requirements
- Manufacturer recommendations where applicable
- Historical defects
- Environmental conditions
- Operating conditions
- Maintenance intervals
- Previous maintenance results
- Risk and priority

A structured preventive maintenance program supports asset reliability and helps reduce unexpected deterioration or failures.

## Documentation Control

Technical maintenance documentation should be controlled so that field teams use current and approved information.

Important controls include:

- Document identification
- Revision number
- Effective date
- Approval status
- Responsible function
- Distribution control
- Revision history
- Obsolete document control

Proper document control reduces the risk of outdated procedures being used during maintenance activities.

## Roles & Responsibilities

Maintenance procedures should establish clear responsibilities between relevant functions.

### Maintenance Management

- Maintenance planning
- Resource coordination
- Work prioritization
- Performance monitoring
- Technical decision coordination

### Field Supervisor

- Work preparation
- Team coordination
- Safety implementation
- Work monitoring
- Quality verification

### Maintenance Personnel

- Execute assigned work
- Follow approved procedures
- Identify abnormal conditions
- Report defects and unsafe conditions
- Maintain work area safety

### Supporting Functions

- Technical review
- Material coordination
- Documentation control
- Safety support
- Administrative support

## Safety & Emergency Response

Maintenance procedures should include appropriate emergency response provisions.

Potential emergency scenarios may include:

- Personnel falling
- Electrical shock
- Falling components or panels
- Extreme weather
- Foreign material entering the railway track
- Equipment failure
- Unsafe working conditions

A general response sequence is:

Stop Work  
↓  
Secure the Work Area  
↓  
Protect Personnel  
↓  
Notify Relevant Operational Control  
↓  
Emergency Response / Evacuation  
↓  
Incident Reporting  
↓  
Investigation & Corrective Action

Emergency response requirements should be aligned with applicable operational and safety procedures.
