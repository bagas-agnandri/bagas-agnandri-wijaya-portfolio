## Hazard Identification

Hazard identification should be performed before work begins and reviewed when working conditions change.

Typical railway maintenance hazards include:

- Railway movement
- Working at height
- Falling objects
- Electrical energy
- Manual handling
- Tools and equipment
- Slips, trips, and falls
- Extreme weather
- Poor visibility
- Restricted access areas
- Vehicle and equipment movement
- Material entering the railway track
- Structural or component instability

Hazard identification should consider the actual work method, environment, personnel exposure, equipment, and operational conditions.

---

## Risk Assessment

Risk assessment may consider:

- Likelihood of occurrence
- Severity of potential consequences
- Existing controls
- Additional control requirements
- Work environment
- Personnel exposure
- Changes in working conditions

A simplified risk assessment process is:

Hazard  
↓  
Potential Consequence  
↓  
Likelihood  
↓  
Severity  
↓  
Risk Level  
↓  
Control Measures  
↓  
Residual Risk

The assessment should be reviewed when there are significant changes to work scope, environment, equipment, or operating conditions.

---

## Risk Control Hierarchy

Risk controls should prioritize measures that eliminate or reduce hazards at their source.

A general hierarchy is:

1. Elimination
2. Substitution
3. Engineering Controls
4. Administrative Controls
5. Personal Protective Equipment

PPE should support other controls rather than being treated as the only form of protection.

## Sample Railway Maintenance Risk Assessment

| Activity | Hazard | Potential Consequence | Key Control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trackside inspection | Railway movement | Serious injury | Work protection and communication |
| Sound barrier repair | Working at height | Fall from height | Fall protection and safe access |
| Panel replacement | Falling material | Injury / property damage | Exclusion zone and material control |
| Electrical work | Electrical energy | Electric shock | Isolation and authorized personnel |
| Material handling | Manual handling | Musculoskeletal injury | Proper lifting technique / mechanical aid |
| Outdoor maintenance | Extreme weather | Injury / reduced work safety | Weather monitoring and work suspension |
| Night work | Poor visibility | Collision / injury | Adequate lighting and high-visibility PPE |
| Field activity | Unsafe condition | Injury / incident | Stop-work authority |

All examples in this table are generalized and do not represent actual company risk assessment data.

## Trackside Safety

Activities conducted near railway tracks require appropriate operational coordination and work protection.

Key considerations include:

- Work authorization
- Work area protection
- Communication with relevant operational control
- Defined working limits
- Personnel awareness
- Material and equipment control
- Safe access and egress
- Emergency communication
- Stop-work authority

Maintenance activities should not continue when required safety or operational controls are not adequately established.

## Emergency Response

Maintenance procedures should include emergency response arrangements for foreseeable scenarios.

Potential emergency situations may include:

- Personnel falling
- Electrical shock
- Falling panel or component
- Extreme weather
- Fire
- Equipment failure
- Material entering the railway track
- Serious injury
- Unsafe infrastructure condition

↓

↓

↓  
Emergency Response / Evacuation  
↓  
Incident Reporting  
↓  
Investigation  
↓

Emergency procedures should be aligned with applicable operational, safety, and emergency management requirements.

## Stop-Work Authority

Personnel should have the ability to stop work when an unsafe condition is identified.

Examples include:

- Loss of required work protection
- Extreme weather
- Unsafe access
- Uncontrolled falling-object risk
- Electrical hazard
- Equipment failure
- Unexpected railway operational conditions
- Structural instability
- Any condition presenting an unacceptable risk

Work should only resume after the hazard has been addressed and required controls have been re-established.

---

## Safety Monitoring

Safety performance should be monitored during work execution.

Monitoring may include:

- Compliance with work procedures
- PPE compliance
- Work area condition
- Safe use of tools and equipment
- Work-at-height controls
- Housekeeping
- Personnel behavior
- Environmental conditions
- Implementation of identified control measures

Supervision should identify unsafe conditions early and initiate corrective actions.
