# K3 / Risk Management

## Overview

This case study presents a structured approach to occupational health and safety risk management for railway infrastructure maintenance activities.

The approach covers hazard identification, risk assessment, risk control, safe work planning, emergency response, work monitoring, and continuous improvement.

The content is presented using generalized and simulated information for professional portfolio purposes.

---

## Professional Context

Railway infrastructure maintenance involves various hazards associated with field activities, railway operations, work at height, tools and equipment, electrical systems, environmental conditions, material handling, and emergency situations.

My professional experience includes supporting the implementation of safety controls during railway maintenance activities, identifying potential hazards, considering associated risks, and ensuring that field activities are performed according to applicable safety requirements.

Safety is treated as an integral part of maintenance planning and execution.

## Hazard Identification

Hazard identification should be performed before work begins and reviewed when working conditions change.

Typical hazards in railway infrastructure maintenance may include:

- Railway movement
- Working at height
- Falling objects
- Electrical energy
- Manual handling
- Tools and equipment
- Slips, trips, and falls
- Extreme weather
- Poor visibility
- Confined or restricted access areas
- Vehicle and equipment movement
- Material entering the railway track
- Structural or component instability

---

## Risk Assessment

Risk assessment may consider:

- Likelihood of occurrence
- Severity of potential consequences
- Existing controls
- Additional control requirements
- Work environment
- Personnel exposure
- Changes in working conditions

A simplified risk assessment process is:

Hazard  
↓  
Potential Consequence  
↓  
Likelihood  
↓  
Severity  
↓  
Risk Level  
↓  
Control Measures  
↓  
Residual Risk

The assessment should be reviewed when there are significant changes to the work scope, environment, equipment, or operating conditions.

---

## Risk Control Hierarchy

Risk controls should prioritize measures that eliminate or reduce the hazard at its source.

A general hierarchy is:

1. Elimination
2. Substitution
3. Engineering Controls
4. Administrative Controls
5. Personal Protective Equipment

PPE should support other controls rather than being treated as the only form of protection.

## Sample Railway Maintenance Risk Assessment

| Activity | Hazard | Potential Consequence | Key Control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trackside inspection | Railway movement | Serious injury | Work protection and communication |
| Sound barrier repair | Working at height | Fall from height | Fall protection and safe access |
| Panel replacement | Falling material | Injury / property damage | Exclusion zone and material control |
| Electrical work | Electrical energy | Electric shock | Isolation and authorized personnel |
| Material handling | Manual handling | Musculoskeletal injury | Proper lifting technique / mechanical aid |
| Outdoor maintenance | Extreme weather | Injury / reduced work safety | Weather monitoring and work suspension |
| Night work | Poor visibility | Collision / injury | Adequate lighting and high-visibility PPE |
| Field activity | Unsafe condition | Injury / incident | Stop-work authority |

All examples in this table are generalized and do not represent actual company risk assessment data.

## Control Measures

Risk controls should be established before work begins.

Typical controls may include:

### Engineering Controls

- Physical barriers
- Guarding
- Safe access systems
- Fall protection systems
- Mechanical lifting equipment

### Administrative Controls

- Work permits
- Work instructions
- Toolbox meetings
- Competency requirements
- Work scheduling
- Communication procedures
- Inspection and supervision

### Personal Protective Equipment

Depending on the activity:

- Safety helmet
- Safety footwear
- High-visibility clothing
- Safety gloves
- Eye protection
- Hearing protection
- Fall protection equipment

The required controls should be determined according to the specific hazards and applicable safety requirements.

## Trackside Safety

Activities conducted near railway tracks require appropriate operational coordination and work protection.

Key considerations include:

- Work authorization
- Work area protection
- Communication with relevant operational control
- Defined working limits
- Personnel awareness
- Material and equipment control
- Safe access and egress
- Emergency communication
- Stop-work authority

No maintenance activity should continue when required safety or operational controls are not adequately established.

## Emergency Response

Maintenance procedures should include emergency response arrangements for foreseeable scenarios.

Potential emergency situations may include:

- Personnel falling
- Electrical shock
- Falling panel or component
- Extreme weather
- Fire
- Equipment failure
- Material entering the railway track
- Serious injury
- Unsafe infrastructure condition

A general emergency response sequence is:

Incident  
↓  
STOP WORK  
↓  
Secure the Area  
↓  
Protect Personnel  
↓  
Notify Relevant Operational Control  
↓  
Emergency Response / Evacuation  
↓  
Incident Reporting  
↓  
Investigation  
↓  
Corrective Action

Emergency procedures should be aligned with the applicable operational, safety, and emergency management requirements.

---

## Stop-Work Authority

Personnel should have the ability to stop work when an unsafe condition is identified.

Examples of conditions that may require work suspension include:

- Loss of required work protection
- Extreme weather
- Unsafe access
- Uncontrolled falling-object risk
- Electrical hazard
- Equipment failure
- Unexpected railway operational conditions
- Structural instability
- Any condition presenting an unacceptable risk

Work should only resume after the hazard has been addressed and the required controls have been re-established.

---

## Safety Monitoring

Safety performance should be monitored during work execution.

Monitoring may include:

- Compliance with work procedures
- PPE compliance
- Work area condition
- Safe use of tools and equipment
- Work-at-height controls
- Housekeeping
- Personnel behavior
- Environmental conditions
- Implementation of identified control measures

Supervision should identify unsafe conditions early and initiate corrective actions.
